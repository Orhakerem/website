Booking an apartment directly is not complicated, but it does move a few checks from a platform's interface to you. Nobody else is standing between you and the host, so it is worth knowing three things: how to confirm that the host is who they say they are, how to write a request that gets a precise answer, and how to read the confirmation before you commit.

This guide is about that process. It is not a comparison of direct booking against platforms — if that is the decision you are still weighing, read [Book Direct vs. Booking Platforms in Tel Aviv](/blog/book-direct-vs-airbnb-or-hakerem) first. Here we assume you have decided to contact a host directly and want to do it well.

## Step 1: Verify that the host is genuine

Most problems with direct rentals do not come from real hosts. They come from copied listings, look-alike websites and messages that impersonate a property. A few minutes of checking removes almost all of that risk.

### Find the host yourself, not through a link someone sent you

Search for the property by name and reach its website or business profile on your own. If a message, an advert or a stranger in a group chat sends you a link, do not use it as your starting point. Type the address yourself and compare.

For Or Hakerem, the official website is orhakerem.com, and the business is listed on [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?cid=11119085925362597877) with its address in Kerem HaTeimanim and public guest reviews. Contact details that do not match the ones published on the official site are a reason to stop and ask.

### Check that the details agree with each other

A genuine host leaves a consistent trail. The street address on the website should match the map pin. The photographs on the property page should show the same rooms, views and furniture as the photographs on the map listing and in guest reviews. The name of the business should be the same everywhere.

If you want a quick extra check, run one or two of the photographs through a reverse image search. A property's own photos usually appear on its own site, its map listing and the platforms it uses. Photos that also appear under a different name in another city are a warning sign.

### Be careful with pressure

Real hosts answer questions. They do not need you to decide in the next ten minutes, and they do not refuse to put details in writing. Be cautious if you are pushed to pay immediately to "hold" dates, asked to use a payment method you would never normally use, or told that a written quote is unnecessary. A rate that is far below every comparable apartment in the same area and season deserves the same caution.

None of this is meant to make you suspicious of every host. It is simply the same care you would take with any large purchase made at a distance.

## Step 2: Write a request that gets a precise answer

Once you know who you are talking to, the quality of the answer depends largely on the quality of the question. A vague "is it available in March?" produces a vague reply and several rounds of messages. A complete request usually gets a complete answer the first time.

### Choose the apartment before you write

Read the property pages properly before sending anything. At Or Hakerem, the [properties page](/properties) sets out the two apartments: a penthouse with outdoor space for larger groups, and a more compact, central apartment. Decide which one fits — or say clearly that you are unsure and why — so the reply can be about your stay rather than a general description.

Location matters too. If you are still deciding where in the city to stay, our guide to [where to stay in Tel Aviv](/blog/where-to-stay-in-tel-aviv-neighborhoods-guide) explains how the central neighbourhoods differ.

### Include the details that change the answer

A useful request contains:

- the apartment you are interested in;
- your exact check-in and check-out dates, and whether they are flexible;
- the number of adults and children;
- your expected arrival time, especially if it is late at night or close to Shabbat;
- the purpose of the stay if it matters — work, a family visit, a celebration;
- anything that could affect the stay, such as accessibility needs or luggage storage.

Here is a short example you can adapt:

> Hello, we are two adults and one child looking at the penthouse from 12 to 17 April. Our flight lands at 22:30, so we expect to arrive around midnight. Could you confirm availability, send the full total for those dates, and explain how late arrival works? We could shift by one day if that helps.

That message answers most of the host's questions before they are asked. You can send it through the [reservation page](/reservation), which already asks for the dates and apartment.

### Mention longer stays in the first message

If you are staying for a month or more, say so straight away. Longer stays raise different practical questions — workspace, laundry, how cleaning works over several weeks — and the conversation is easier when the host knows the context from the start. Our [long-term and monthly stays guide](/blog/long-term-monthly-stays-tel-aviv) lists the questions worth asking.

## Step 3: Read the confirmation before you commit

The reply to your request is a quote, not a reservation. Before you treat the dates as secured, read what you have received as carefully as you would read any contract.

### What the confirmation should state

Check that it names:

- the apartment and its address;
- the check-in and check-out dates, written out;
- the number of guests;
- the full total, including any fees that apply, in a stated currency;
- what happens next, and by when;
- the cancellation terms, or where to find them.

If any of these is missing, ask for it to be added. This is a normal request and a reliable host will not mind it.

### Read it slowly, once

Do not approve a confirmation while boarding a plane or between meetings. Read it once in full, compare the dates against your flights, and make sure the guest count includes everyone. Most booking misunderstandings are not disputes; they are a date typed one day off or a child who was never mentioned.

A useful test is to try explaining the booking to someone else in a minute: where you are staying, when you arrive, what the total covers and who to contact. If you cannot, something is still unclear.

### Keep it where your whole group can find it

Save the confirmation, the address and the arrival instructions in one place, and share them with anyone who may arrive before you. This matters most when travellers land at different times or when the person who booked is not the first to arrive.

## Before you travel

In the days before arrival, check that the host still has a way to reach you and that your arrival time has not changed. If it has, send a short message. A note sent two days before travel is much easier to deal with than a question at the door at midnight.

If you are arriving close to Shabbat or a holiday, confirm the arrival arrangements early, since some services in the city change their hours.

## A short checklist

Before you confirm any direct booking, you should be able to tick every line:

1. I found the host's website and contact details myself, and they match the map listing.
2. The photographs, address and business name are consistent across sources.
3. Nobody is pressuring me to pay before I have the details in writing.
4. My request stated the apartment, exact dates, guest count and arrival time.
5. The confirmation shows the address, dates, guests, full total and cancellation terms.
6. Everyone in my group has the arrival details.

If every line is ticked, you have done what a platform would otherwise have done for you, and you can look forward to the trip.
