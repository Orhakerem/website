Tel Aviv has become one of the Mediterranean's most popular bases for remote workers, slow travelers, and people relocating or visiting family for a month or more. If you're planning an extended stay, here's how to do it well — and why a direct, flexible rental beats both hotels and rigid platform bookings. If your trip is shorter than a month, our guide to [short-term rentals in Tel Aviv](/blog/short-term-seasonal-rentals-tel-aviv) covers the different questions that apply.

## Why Stay Longer in Tel Aviv?

A few days isn't enough to know Tel Aviv. Staying a month or more lets you settle into a neighborhood, find your café and your beach spot, and live at the city's actual rhythm — slow mornings, long lunches, and the famous Saturday-night energy after Shabbat ends.

Tel Aviv works particularly well for extended stays because it's compact, walkable, beach-facing, and has a strong remote-work culture: fast internet, abundant cafés, and a large international community.

## What to Look for in a Long-Stay Apartment

**A real kitchen.** For a month, you'll want to cook. A fully equipped kitchen near a fresh market (like the Carmel Market, 400m away) changes the economics and the experience of a long stay.

**A practical work setup.** A comfortable place to work and reliable internet matter for remote workers. Confirm the details that are important for your work before you book.

**A genuine neighborhood.** Staying in a residential area rather than a hotel strip means you live among locals, with bakeries, laundromats, and grocery stores on your doorstep. Kerem HaTeimanim is exactly this — central but residential. (See our [neighborhood guide](/blog/kerem-hateimanim-neighborhood-guide).)

**Flexibility and a real host.** Long stays inevitably involve changes — an extra week, a mid-stay clean, a delivery. A direct relationship with a responsive host makes all of this easy.
